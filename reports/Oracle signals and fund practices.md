# Oracle should borrow Citadel's discipline, not its leverage

Oracle is a one-person US equity system in which Claude reads and proposes while deterministic code sizes and gates. The parts of Citadel's model it can copy are the controls, not the returns engine. Citadel, Millennium and Point72 make money from three things. An independent risk group strips out market, sector and style exposure. Capital moves quickly away from losing books under hard drawdown stops. Hundreds of roughly uncorrelated pods are financed at about **12x gross-to-net leverage**. Oracle can reproduce the first two cheaply in code, the first by measuring and limiting those exposures rather than hedging them away. It cannot reproduce the third at all. On signals, the free-data evidence is strongest for slow, low-turnover signals built from EDGAR XBRL: profitability/quality, net share issuance and asset growth. Volatility-managed 12-1 momentum, opportunistic insider purchases from Form 4, and year-over-year text change in 10-Ks ("Lazy Prices") come next. Assume every published premium loses roughly half its strength after publication, and that much of it sits in microcaps and short legs Oracle will not trade. Post-earnings drift has largely disappeared for large caps since about 2006. Copying 13Fs, business-cycle sector rotation, and LLM agents that forecast or trade on their own all lack credible after-cost support. LLMs add measurable value as grounded extractors and classifiers whose numbers code recomputes, not as forecasters. Their documented failure modes are memorization and look-ahead, hallucinated figures, and prompt injection through the documents they read. The current stack (Python, a WAL-mode SQLite system of record, FastAPI) suits the scale. Add a read-only DuckDB layer now and versioned Parquet snapshots once backtests become routine; Postgres is unnecessary. Two items matter more than any framework: a bitemporal point-in-time data model and an immutable proposal-evidence record. This report synthesizes research notes and is not financial advice.

## Pod platforms run on three mechanisms, and Oracle can copy two

### Central risk strips factor exposure so that only stock-specific return is rewarded

A pod at a multi-manager platform is a portfolio manager and a small team running a separately allocated book. Technology, risk, operations and financing are shared. Citadel's equity side is organised as sector-focused, bottom-up teams "in a market-neutral framework" ([Citadel Global Equities](https://www.citadel.com/what-we-do/equities/citadel-global-equities/)). Its Portfolio Construction & Risk Group is independent of the investment teams and reports directly to the CEO, and risk analysts sit inside each fundamental equities business ([Hedge Fund Insight](http://www.hedgefundinsight.org/risk-management-at-citadel/)). The most specific public description of the rules comes from CRO Joanna Welsh in a Risk.net interview. Equity portfolios must be **beta-neutral** and must carry a **minimum level of idiosyncratic variance**. A team of more than 50 keeps Citadel's proprietary factor model under review by comparing the risk it predicted with the risk that was realised ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/awards/7955800/hedge-fund-of-the-year-citadel)). That interview is around 2021 and may be dated. The commercial reference model, MSCI Barra USE4, uses 60 GICS-based industries, 12 style factors and one country factor ([MSCI USE4](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/242721/Barra_US_Equity_Model_USE4.pdf)). Oracle can approximate that structure with free data: SPY, sector ETFs and a handful of style factors. Secondary sources also quote specific neutrality bands, such as beta within ±5% and style exposure under 0.5 standard deviations, and gross exposure of 200-600% ([hedgefundinterview.com](https://hedgefundinterview.com/multi-manager-hedge-funds); [Resonanz Capital](https://resonanzcapital.com/insights/what-the-hedge-leaves-behind)). No primary source confirms any of these numbers.

### Drawdown ladders and fast reallocation are the downside half of a tournament

The best-documented stop rule is Millennium's. A WSJ report, relayed by eFinancialCareers, says PMs who lose **5% of allocated capital typically have it halved**. PMs say capital is often pulled at -2.5% to -3%, and a further 2.5% loss usually means being fired. Enforcement is not mechanical, though: senior PMs have survived repeated losses ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/pratik-madhvani-glen-scheinberg)). The widely repeated **7.5% termination level** appears only in secondary blogs ([Navnoor Bawa](https://www.navnoorbawaresearch.com/p/millennium-and-balyasnys-stop-loss)). Millennium's and Balyasny's 2026 ADV brochures reportedly say only that they "allocate and reallocate" capital among PMs ([youngandcalculated](https://youngandcalculated.substack.com/p/how-multi-manager-hedge-funds-actually)). PMs keep roughly 15-22.5% of their book's net P&L ([hedgefundinterview.com](https://hedgefundinterview.com/pod-pm-compensation); [Resonanz](https://resonanzcapital.com/insights/the-cost-of-alpha-generation)). The standard measure of skill net of factor exposure is the appraisal (information) ratio, residual alpha divided by residual volatility ([Duke lecture notes](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~boller/Econ.471-571.F19/Lec6_471-571_F19.pdf)). No firm publishes how it weights Sharpe, hit rate or win/loss ratio. The model's weakness is that pods share risk models and stop rules, so they de-gross together. Pods were closed quickly in the August 2024 turmoil ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-08-30/bluecrest-millennium-pods-faced-quick-closure-after-turmoil)), trade press called March 2026 a rare synchronized drawdown across the "Big Three" ([HedgeCo](https://www.hedgeco.net/news/04/2026/march-malaise-results-are-in-critical-stress-test-of-the-modern-pod-based-hedge-fund-model.html)), and a crowded momentum/AI unwind followed in July 2026 ([Artificial Finance](https://artificialfinance.org/2026/08/the-ai-momentum-unwind/)).

### Leverage, breadth and staff are what Oracle cannot copy

The returns engine depends on scale. Office of Financial Research Form PF data, as summarised secondhand, puts average multi-strategy gross-to-net leverage at **9.51x in September 2023 and 12.29x in June 2026** ([youngandcalculated](https://youngandcalculated.substack.com/p/how-multi-manager-hedge-funds-actually)). Bloomberg found that Citadel's three largest funds accrued almost **$12.5B in pass-through fees** from early 2022 to September 2024, mostly compensation ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2025-hedge-fund-investment-fees/)). Cambridge Associates puts the median platform pass-through at **4.53% of assets** ([Cambridge Associates](https://publishedresearch.cambridgeassociates.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2025-10-Hedge-Fund-Discretionary-Expense-Summary-2024.pdf)). Point72 runs more than 200 investing teams ([hedgefundinterview.com](https://hedgefundinterview.com/major-pod-shops-2026)). The platform's Sharpe ratio comes from diversifying across those pods, and a market-neutral, low-volatility book only pays at that leverage with cheap financing and borrow. Oracle is one pod with retail margin and borrow costs. Copying Citadel's leverage or scale is therefore not possible, and a scaled-down imitation of a levered market-neutral book would most likely lose money after financing costs. Griffin himself locates Citadel's edge in "better predictive analytics, better risk management techniques" ([Hedge Fund Alpha, SALT 2016](https://hedgefundalpha.com/conferences/salt-2016-ken-griffin/)) and in data quality ([S&P Global podcast](https://www.spglobal.com/en/research-insights/podcasts/leaders/ken-griffin-on-building-citadel-ai-and-independent-thinking)). On AI in investing, he said in 2025, "I can't say it's been game-changing" ([AOL/Business Insider](https://www.aol.com/citadel-founder-ken-griffin-says-083015901.html)).

What transfers is process. Give each idea a pre-committed risk budget. Apply a book-level drawdown ladder, measured on benchmark-relative return for a long-biased book. Measure skill as residual return after SPY, sector and style exposure. Back-test the risk model's predictions against realised risk. Keep a journal of each thesis, catalyst and invalidation point. Run simple central checks: net beta, sector concentration, crowding proxies and clustering of earnings dates. Because nobody can fire Oracle's PM, the stops must be mechanical and enforced in code. Oracle also has advantages a pod does not. No allocator or prime broker forces it to de-gross, it can hold longer, and it can be a patient buyer when pod stop-outs dump crowded names. Those advantages apply only inside its own limits: when the drawdown ladder or volatility target cuts risk, that rule beats any plan to buy the unwind. One caveat: no study was found that tests pod-style drawdown ladders on retail long-only books, so the ladder is a discipline device, not a proven return enhancer.

## Slow EDGAR signals beat fast ones after decay and costs

### Every published premium shrinks, and the shrinkage is predictable

Three peer-reviewed results set expectations. McLean & Pontiff (JF 2016) study 97 predictors and find returns about **26% lower out of sample and about 58% lower after publication** ([CFA Digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2016/06/does-academic-research-destroy-stock-return-predictability-digest-summary)). The working paper reported about 35% ([Ivey WP](https://ivey.uwo.ca/media/3775549/pontiff.pdf)), so check which version a figure comes from. Hou, Xue & Zhang (RFS 2020) use NYSE breakpoints and value weights and find **65% of 452 anomalies fail** a 1.96 t-hurdle. That includes 96% of trading-friction anomalies, and the failure rate rises to 82% at t = 2.78 ([Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/replicating-anomalies/)). Novy-Marx & Velikov (RFS 2016) find that most anomalies with **one-sided monthly turnover below 50%** keep significant net spreads, few faster ones do, and a buy/hold band is the best simple cost control ([Novy-Marx PDF](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf)). Against the pessimists, Chen & Zimmermann (Critical Finance Review 2022) reproduce published predictors with about 98% of the clearly significant ones keeping t > 1.96 ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/open-source-cross-sectional-asset-pricing.htm)). Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen (JF 2023) find most of 153 factors replicate globally ([NBER w28432](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28432)). These results do not conflict. Factors usually replicate in sample, but replication does not mean they survive costs, publication, or the exclusion of microcaps. Two further problems hit Oracle directly. Anomaly profits come disproportionately from the short leg, which a long-biased book forgoes (Stambaugh, Yu & Yuan, a background-knowledge claim not re-verified in this session). And free XBRL data begins in 2009-2011, so an accounting backtest gets only about 15 years of history. That window falls after publication for accruals, the F-score, net issuance and asset growth, which makes it useful out-of-sample evidence for them. It is not cleanly out of sample for gross profitability (published 2013) or quality minus junk (published 2019).

Accounting signals rank first for four reasons. They can be built entirely from free XBRL. They rebalance about once a year, which keeps them well under the 50%-turnover survival line. Gross profitability works in large stocks, although asset growth is much weaker value-weighted. And the profitability and investment categories replicate better than the friction anomalies under Hou-Xue-Zhang's tests. The catch is that the original effect sizes for gross profitability, net issuance, asset growth, accruals and the F-score were **not re-verified in this session**, so this report quotes none of them. Accruals has reportedly largely decayed since the 2000s, and the F-score works mainly in small, thinly covered value stocks (both background-knowledge claims). Treat both as low priority. Chen & Zimmermann's open-source library provides reference implementations and free monthly long-short returns for about 205 predictors ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/open-source-cross-sectional-asset-pricing.htm)). Oracle should check its own builds against them.

Insider purchases and Lazy Prices are the most distinctive filing signals, and both suit an LLM-assisted pipeline. The insider effect comes almost entirely from **opportunistic** insiders. The paper classifies only insiders with at least one trade in each of the prior three years. Those who traded in the same calendar month for three straight years are routine, and the rest of that universe is opportunistic, so a first-time buyer falls outside the tested sample. A long-biased book should plan on the 72 bp value-weighted buys-only alpha, not the 82 bp long-short headline. Routine buys minus sells earn -20 bp value-weighted and an insignificant 43 bp equal-weighted. The gap between the value-weighted and equal-weighted figures shows the effect is concentrated in small stocks. Expect much less in large and mid caps, where CEO purchases are rare. Lazy Prices has the largest headline number but the weakest out-of-sample record: no independent post-2014 replication was found, and the signal is now sold by vendors. Its value to Oracle is partly as a risk flag. Language changes in the risk-factor and litigation sections predict bad news and bankruptcies.

### Market and macro signals are better as risk dials than return forecasts

Momentum is among the best-replicated anomalies, but its crashes are partly forecastable. They arrive in rebounds after market declines and when volatility is high. Volatility scaling (Daniel-Moskowitz; Barroso-Santa-Clara) is the published mitigation and needs only daily prices ([NBER w20439](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439)). The 52-week-high variant is peer-reviewed (George & Hwang, JF 2004, via [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/the-secret-to-momentum-is-the-52-week-high/)). Time-series momentum on a single index has thin support. Huang, Li, Wang & Zhou (JFE 2020) find "little evidence of TSM" asset by asset ([WUSTL](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/time-series-momentum-is-it-there/)), and the case for a 200-day moving-average rule rests on practitioner work that shows lower drawdowns rather than higher returns. Low-volatility and betting-against-beta are contested. Novy-Marx & Velikov (JFE 2022) show the published construction is effectively equal-weighted, with a 0.996 correlation ([BABAB PDF](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/BABAB.pdf)), and a long-only book cannot apply the leverage that makes it work anyway.

At the market level, aggregate short interest is "arguably the strongest known predictor of aggregate stock returns." Rapach, Ringgenberg & Zhou (JFE 2016) report an annual-horizon out-of-sample R² of 13.24% in a 1973-2013 sample ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cuf:wpaper:716)). However, the index is detrended over the full sample, so it must be rebuilt with expanding windows, and it underperformed in 2000-2007 ([CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/short-selling/aggregate-short-interest-and-future-stock-market-returns)). The Fed's free monthly excess bond premium (EBP) mainly predicts **recessions and economic activity**, not equity returns ([FEDS Notes](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/updating-the-recession-risk-and-the-excess-bond-premium-20161006.html)). It is re-estimated each month, so Oracle must archive its own vintages. Rising oil predicts lower stock returns about a month ahead across developed markets (Driesprong et al., JFE 2008; [Erasmus](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/1017)), but post-2008 performance was not verified. The pre-FOMC drift once accounted for about 80% of excess equity returns since 1994 ([NY Fed SR512](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr512.pdf)). Since 2011 it has survived only around press conferences and shrank to about 22 bp ([BIS WP 1079](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1079.pdf)). Even with perfect foresight of NBER cycle phases, business-cycle sector rotation added about 2.3% a year, and realistic timing errors removed significance (Stangl, Jacobsen & Visaltanachoti, working paper, via [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/economic-indicators/perfect-sector-rotation)). The peer-reviewed follow-up agrees that the outperformance "quickly diminishes" after costs and timing errors ([Molchanov & Stangl, IJFE 2023](https://mro.massey.ac.nz/handle/10179/69590)).

Free VIX and Cboe put/call series support only index-level signals with thin evidence. The best stock-level option signal, the implied-volatility smirk (Xing, Zhang & Zhao, JFQA 2010), needs paid per-stock implied volatilities ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v45y2010i03p641-662_00.html)). Oracle should use VIX, credit spreads and the EBP to scale gross exposure and tighten risk, not to forecast returns.

## Combine equally, validate harshly, size on risk

### Equal-weighted z-scores beat optimised weights out of sample

Estimation error dominates signal weighting. DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal (RFS 2009) tested 14 optimised portfolio models, and none consistently beat 1/N out of sample. For 25 assets, sample mean-variance needs more than **3,000 months** of data to win ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v22y2009i5p1915-1953.html)). Combining forecasts helps mainly because averaging reduces variance (Rapach, Strauss & Zhou, RFS 2010, [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v23y2010i2p821-862.html)). AQR found that value-spread factor timing "fail[s] to produce economically meaningful improvement" ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/journal-article/contrarian-factor-timing-is-deceptively-difficult)). These are analogies, since no study was retrieved that compares weighting schemes for cross-sectional stock signals directly. Still, the defensible recipe is clear:

1. Winsorize and rank-normalise each signal cross-sectionally.
2. Residualise it against beta, sector, log size and 12-1 momentum, so a "new" signal cannot be a disguised momentum bet. Leave momentum itself out of the momentum control, or the sleeve residualises to zero.
3. Measure rank IC at 1, 5, 21 and 63 days to find the decay curve.
4. Equal-weight the residual z-scores, shrinking any IC-based weights heavily toward equal.

The fundamental law (IR ≈ IC × √breadth, scaled by a transfer coefficient for long-only constraints) sets expectations, not forecasts. Naive breadth inflates the implied IR because stocks share factor exposures ([New Frontier Advisors](https://newfrontieradvisors.com/media/1744/fundamental-law-september-2019.pdf)).

### A validation gate built on a trial log

Harvey, Liu & Zhu (RFS 2016) argue a new factor needs **t > 3.0** ([NBER w20592](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592)). The Deflated Sharpe Ratio corrects a backtest for the number of trials and for non-normal returns ([SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)). The Probability of Backtest Overfitting, estimated by combinatorially symmetric cross-validation, measures how often the in-sample winner ranks below the median out of sample ([SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)). Both statistics need the **count of every variant tried**, so the trial log is a required input, not bookkeeping. A synthesised minimum standard for Oracle, which no single source prescribes:

1. Pre-register the hypothesis, universe, horizon and cost assumptions.
2. Log every variant.
3. Use point-in-time, survivorship-aware data with delisting returns. Where they are missing, the convention is -30% for NYSE/Amex ([Shumway 1997](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v52y1997i1p327-40.html)) and -55% for Nasdaq ([Shumway & Warther 1999](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v54y1999i6p2361-2379.html)) performance delistings.
4. Require t ≥ 3 for a novel signal, or about 2 for a replicated published one, plus a DSR of at least 0.95.
5. Run CSCV across the logged variants and reject if PBO exceeds about 0.5. Then touch an untouched holdout only once.
6. Require the signal to survive realistic costs with a buy/hold band.
7. Size as though live edge will be about 40-50% of the backtest, in line with McLean-Pontiff's published 58% decline. The working paper's 35% would imply 65%.
8. Run a short dry-run period to catch data and timing bugs, not to prove edge.

With tens to hundreds of names and realistic ICs of 0.02-0.05, reaching t = 3 takes years. Treating monthly cross-sections as independent, 100 names need about 3 years at an IC of 0.05 and about 19 years at 0.02, which is longer than free XBRL history. That alone argues for published, economically motivated signals over anything data-mined.

### Inverse-volatility sizing, a volatility target and fractional Kelly at most

Kelly sizing is fragile when expected returns are poorly estimated: "it is possible, through bad scenarios, to lose most of one's wealth" ([MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Good_Bad_Kelly.pdf)). Portfolio-level volatility targeting raises Sharpe ratios for equities and consistently reduces extreme returns and drawdowns ([Harvey et al., JPM 2018](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)). Volatility *timing* for alpha (Moreira-Muir) failed to beat unmanaged portfolios in real time in most of 103 strategies, because the scaling parameters drifted ([Cederburg et al., JFE 2020](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)). Momentum was among the exceptions, which supports scaling Oracle's momentum sleeve. The robust default is a weight proportional to a capped score divided by volatility, roughly equal risk contributions, single-name caps, and a book volatility target. Use at most 0.25-0.5 of Kelly on a haircut expected return. The specific numbers are practitioner conventions, not sourced findings. Stops should be k × σ × √horizon, so that a slow signal is not stopped out before it can work. No retrieved study shows that ATR stops add value to cross-sectional signals. The holding period should match the IC-decay peak, and expected annual costs should stay below about a third of expected gross alpha.

### The proposal record is what makes outcomes attributable

Harvey, Rattray, Sinclair & Van Hemert found that discretionary and systematic equity hedge funds had similar alpha once volatility and factor exposures were controlled for ([SSRN 2880641](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2880641)). Skill only becomes visible after factor adjustment. Oracle should therefore write one immutable, timestamped row per proposal, before any outcome is known. Outcomes go in a separate append-only table keyed by proposal_id, so the immutability triggers need no exception. No standard schema was found, so the fields below are synthesised from the validation literature.

| Field group | Contents |
|---|---|
| Identity and point-in-time | proposal_id, created_at, ticker, direction, data_asof, hash of the exact input rows, LLM model version and prompt hash |
| Per-signal evidence | raw value, cross-sectional z/percentile, factor-residualised z, composite weight, signal_version linked to the trial log |
| Claude's view | analyst_score recorded before the composite is shown; thesis text with cited filing spans (accession, section, character offsets) |
| Forecast | composite score, expected excess return, expected volatility, probability (e.g. P(beats sector over 63d) = 0.6) for Brier scoring |
| Timing | horizon_days at the IC-decay peak, catalyst type and date, hold-through-earnings decision |
| Invalidation | price stop (k·σ·√h), fundamental condition, time stop |
| Sizing and risk | target risk, σ estimate, resulting weight, beta, sector, size and momentum loadings at entry, human approval and any override |
| Outcome (separate append-only table, also for rejected and unfilled proposals) | total return, factor-residual return over the same horizon, max adverse excursion, exit reason, costs |

Each month, pool every proposal whose horizon has elapsed, including rejected and unfilled ones scored on shadow outcomes. Pooling only approved trades selects on the scores being tested and compresses their IC. Then compute each signal's realised rank IC against residual return, and regress residual returns on all component scores, with Claude's score as one regressor. When testing a signal that is itself a style factor, such as momentum or quality, strip only market and sector from the return, or the test removes the signal's own payoff. Report standard errors, because 63-day horizons overlap. Until a track record exists, analyst_score is logged but carries no composite weight, which keeps Claude out of the forecasting role the evidence below rejects. If Claude's marginal IC is near zero after the quant signals are included, its judgment is adding noise and should become a veto for data errors and pending corporate actions rather than a weight. Give it weight only once its IC reaches t ≈ 2. Since n ≈ (t/IC)², that takes about 400 independent calls at an IC of 0.10 and about 1,600 at 0.05. Human overrides should be logged as trials too.

## LLMs earn their keep as grounded extractors, not forecasters

### The return evidence is thin, decaying or withdrawn

No LLM return signal reviewed has peer-reviewed, post-training-cutoff, after-cost evidence. The best case is Lopez-Lira & Tang's headline-sentiment signal, still a **working paper** (arXiv v6, October 2025). It predicts next-day returns, mostly in small stocks and on negative news. Cumulative returns of over 500% fall to about 50% at 25 bp per trade, and "strategy returns decline as LLM adoption rises" ([arXiv 2304.07619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619v6)). Kim, Muhn & Nikolaev's financial-statement-analysis paper claimed GPT-4 beat analysts at earnings direction. A search summary of its arXiv listing says it was **withdrawn**, which could not be confirmed directly ([arXiv 2407.17866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17866v3)). Their "Bloated Disclosures" paper was withdrawn after a co-author's replication "did not support the reported findings" ([Nikolaev](https://faculty.chicagobooth.edu/valeri-nikolaev/ongoing-research-projects)). Agentic trading systems fare worst. In "Profit Mirage," returns "evaporate once the model's knowledge window ends" ([arXiv 2510.07920](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07920v1)). In the contamination-free StockBench test (DJIA, March-July 2025), most agents failed to beat buy-and-hold ([arXiv 2510.02209](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02209)). Both are preprints. Claims from S&P Global vendor research that transcript strategies "roughly doubled" benchmarks ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/09/23/how-ai-changing-earnings-call-analysis-stock-picks)), and a Substack Sharpe ratio of 2.26, are marketing-grade evidence.

### Memorization makes pre-cutoff backtests of Oracle's own proposals worthless

GPT-4o recalls pre-cutoff S&P 500 levels and macro figures with over **96% directional accuracy**. Instructions to ignore future data fail, and masking names fails because the model reconstructs the context ([Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu, SSRN 5217505](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=5217505), working paper). Sarkar & Vafa confirm look-ahead in earnings-call risk prediction ([NBER conference PDF](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f205530/f205530.pdf)). The bias depends on the task. For short-horizon headline tone it appears modest: chronologically consistent ChronoBERT matches larger models ([arXiv 2502.21206](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21206v2)), and anonymized headlines outperformed named ones because firm identity *distracts* the model ([Glasserman & Lin](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17322v1)). The implication for Oracle is concrete. Claude's proposals can only be evaluated live, after the deployed model's cutoff, and every model upgrade resets that clean window. Replaying history through Claude produces contaminated evidence by construction.

### Reliability ranks classification over extraction over summarisation over forecasting

On FinanceBench's open-book questions about SEC filings, GPT-4-Turbo with a shared retrieval store answered only 19% correctly and refused 68%. With the full long context it reached 79% correct, and with the exact page supplied it reached 85% ([Patronus AI](https://patronus.ai/announcements/patronus-ai-launches-financebench-the-industrys-first-benchmark-for-llm-performance-on-financial-questions); [arXiv 2311.11944](https://web3.arxiv.org/abs/2311.11944?context=cs)). Retrieval quality matters more than the model. LLM-extracted features fed to a downstream model beat direct LLM prediction of volatility from earnings calls ([ECC Analyzer, arXiv 2404.18470](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.18470)). Oracle's division of labour, with Claude reading and proposing while deterministic code sizes and gates, is the right one. It should be tightened in five ways:

1. Claude emits typed facts (value, unit, period, accession, verbatim span), and code checks each span against the filing and reconciles it to XBRL before any number is used.
2. Claude's job is classification and diffing tasks such as tone, guidance direction, 8-K event type and new risk factors (a Lazy Prices-style diff). Forecasting returns is not its job.
3. Filings and news are untrusted input. Indirect prompt injection succeeded 24-30% of the time against a multi-agent trading system in one 2026 preprint ([arXiv 2608.24069](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24069), arXiv ID unverified), and a large red-teaming competition compromised every model in at least one scenario ([Emergent Mind](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2603.15714)). The reading process must have no path to orders.
4. Low-confidence extractions are routed to abstention and human review, since a refusal is safer than a hallucination.
5. Oracle monitors whether its signals correlate with widely available LLM sentiment. The Bank of England warns that common models produce common positions ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2025/bank-england-warns-higher-market-volatility-from-ai-driven-trading/)).

Anthropic's Citations feature cannot be combined with structured outputs: a request using both returns a 400 error ([Anthropic docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations)). Use two passes (cite, then structure), ask for one cited `field: value` line per fact and parse the lines in code, or have code match the spans itself.

## The stack is right; the data model needs the attention

Python with WAL-mode SQLite as the system of record and FastAPI fits a single-user, daily-bar universe of a few hundred names. That is about 1.5M price rows over 20 years, which pandas alone handles. Postgres is justified only by topology or concurrency, not data volume. The triggers are multiple machines or users, a remotely served dashboard, sustained `SQLITE_BUSY` contention, or wanting the LLM's proposals-only write boundary enforced by database roles. DuckDB's core `sqlite` extension can `ATTACH` the SQLite file and query it directly, with locking delegated to SQLite ([duckdb-sqlite](https://github.com/duckdb/duckdb-sqlite)). That provides window functions and `ASOF JOIN` for point-in-time joins at no operational cost. The extension can also *write*, so attach with `ATTACH 'oracle.db' AS o (TYPE sqlite, READ_ONLY)`. A local test on DuckDB 2.0.0 confirmed that this blocks writes and still sees rows held in the WAL, and that a SQLite trigger fires on an `UPDATE` sent through a writable attach. Do not pass a `file:...?mode=ro` URI to `ATTACH`: in the same test DuckDB took it as a literal filename and created a new, empty database. Do not snapshot the live file with a plain copy either, because the copy missed a committed row still in the WAL. Use SQLite's backup API. Add **Parquet snapshots** produced by `COPY ... TO` once backtests or parameter sweeps run often. Each backtest then records the hash of the snapshot it saw, alongside the input-row hash every proposal already carries. DuckDB 2.0.0 (October 2026), Polars 2.x and pandas 3.1 are all actively released ([PyPI duckdb](https://pypi.org/project/duckdb/); [PyPI polars](https://pypi.org/project/polars/); [PyPI pandas](https://pypi.org/project/pandas/)).

Avoid big frameworks at this scale. backtrader has not released since April 2023 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/backtrader/)). zipline-reloaded is in maintenance mode, pins pandas below 3, and fixed its dividend and reverse-split accounting only in October 2026 ([releases](https://github.com/stefan-jansen/zipline-reloaded/releases)). Qlib is China-oriented and its official dataset is disabled ([microsoft/qlib](https://github.com/microsoft/qlib)). NautilusTrader 2.0 is still a release candidate. Point-in-time correctness is a data problem, not a framework problem. Even QuantConnect's bundled Morningstar fundamentals have gaps for some delisted names ([QC forum](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/14219/fundamental-data-missing-for-delisted-stocks/)).

Oracle's real exposure lies in free data. Fundamentals must be stored bitemporally, with period_end, filed_at/available_at and ingested_at. Use the **first-reported** XBRL value per tag and period, because companyfacts "frames" often carry later restated comparatives. Join on `available_at <= decision_time`. Measure the earnings return (EAR) from the 8-K Item 2.02 acceptance time, and classify after-close releases. XBRL EPS exists only from the 10-Q or 10-K filing date, so using it at the 8-K time is look-ahead. An EPS surprise at that time has to be parsed from the press-release exhibit. Short interest becomes usable only about seven business days after settlement, or five if FINRA's proposed change takes effect. Store unadjusted prices plus a corporate-actions table rather than vendor-adjusted series that change retroactively. Treat any free-price backtest as an upper bound unless delisted names are reconstructed. Survivorship in Tiingo's free tier was not checked in this session, but the notes find free price feeds generally survivor-biased. EDGAR Form 25 and Form 15 filings can identify delistings. If Oracle executes through Schwab, it needs its own dry-run mode, because Schwab's API has no paper trading. The more established schwab-py client has not released since June 2025, while schwabdev released 4.0.0 in August 2026 ([PyPI schwab-py](https://pypi.org/project/schwab-py/); [PyPI schwabdev](https://pypi.org/project/schwabdev/)). Put either behind a thin adapter interface.

## Prioritised plan, and what the evidence does not support

The table orders work by expected value per unit of effort. Infrastructure comes first, because without point-in-time data and a proposal record no signal result can be trusted or attributed.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bitemporal point-in-time store; first-reported XBRL; timestamped events | System | EDGAR submissions + companyfacts APIs | Prevents look-ahead, the most common cause of a false backtest |
| 2 | Immutable proposal-evidence record and monthly residual-IC attribution | System | Internal; SPY + sector ETFs + Ken French / Chen-Zimmermann factor returns | Only route to knowing whether Claude or a signal has skill; update lags in the factor files were not checked, so the monthly run should work from ETFs alone |
| 3 | Trial log with DSR/PBO validation gate and holdout | System | Internal | Required inputs for overfitting statistics |
| 4 | Risk-based sizing: inverse volatility, volatility target, name/sector caps, beta check, drawdown ladder on active return | System | Tiingo EOD, ETF proxies | Copies pod discipline; ladder thresholds are discretionary, not proven |
| 5 | LLM grounding: span citations, code recomputation, injection isolation, frozen model versions, live-only evaluation | System | EDGAR text | Addresses hallucination, memorization and injection |
| 6 | Gross profitability / quality composite | Signal | XBRL (revenue, COGS, assets, CFO, NI) | Low turnover, large-cap, high capacity; effect sizes not re-verified |

The evidence does **not** support:

- Copying Citadel's leverage, market-neutral construction or scale. About 12x platform gross-to-net leverage, cheap borrow, hundreds of diversifying pods and multi-billion-dollar talent budgets are unavailable to a single retail account.
- Expecting post-earnings drift in large caps after 2006.
- Copying 13F holdings, which arrive up to 45 days after quarter-end and carry less alpha than the confidential positions that are withheld.
- Business-cycle sector rotation.
- Copper/gold, dollar or public put/call ratios as equity predictors.
- Single-index 200-day trend rules as a return source.
- Pre-FOMC drift as a current signal. After 2011 it survived only at press-conference meetings, every meeting has had a press conference since January 2019, and one paper finds it weakening from 2016 even at those.
- Short-term reversal at retail costs (background knowledge, not re-verified).
- Leveraged betting-against-beta in a long-only book.
- Optimised signal weights or regime-switching weights.
- Full Kelly sizing.
- Volatility timing as an alpha source.
- LLMs forecasting returns or trading on their own.
- Any backtest of Claude's judgment over periods before its training cutoff.

Several figures could not be verified against primary sources in this session. Some are used above with caveats. Others were left out for that reason and are named so they are not reused unchecked:

- **Fund structure:** Millennium's 7.5% termination level, numeric neutrality bands, pod capital sizes, and the OFR leverage figures, which were seen only via a Substack.
- **Signal studies:** McLean-Pontiff's exact published percentages (versions differ). Left out: the Ikenberry et al. buyback figure, Chen-Velikov's net-return estimate, the 10.9%/year smirk spread, the Frazzini-Lamont earnings-premium figure, and the original effect sizes for gross profitability, net issuance and asset growth. The top ranking of those accounting signals therefore rests on background knowledge of the literature.
- **LLM evidence:** the Kim/Muhn/Nikolaev withdrawal, seen through a search snippet; Lopez-Lira & Tang's sample details; the "8 weeks in 2 hours" AI anecdote attributed to Griffin by the Motley Fool; and the arXiv ID of the prompt-injection paper.
- **Regulation and data access:** whether FINRA's proposal for weekly short-interest reporting (SR-FINRA-2026-012) has been approved, and the SEC API rate limits.

Evidence tiers throughout: peer-reviewed journal results (JF, JFE, RFS, CFR, JFQA, JPM) carry the most weight. NBER/SSRN/arXiv working papers (days-to-cover, every LLM return study) are provisional. AQR and Man Group research is practitioner work by authors with a stake in the conclusions. Vendor and Substack claims (sec-api.io, S&P Global, pod-shop blogs) are leads, not evidence.

## Conclusion

Pod platforms are built to make each PM's residual skill visible and to cut off its absence quickly. Oracle's real opportunity is to apply the same machinery to its own analyst, Claude. Two components matter most: a proposal record that captures each signal's residualised score alongside Claude's independent score before outcomes are known, and a monthly attribution job. Over several years of live operation they can answer something no backtest can, which is whether Claude adds information beyond cheap, published factors. A year or two will show only a large effect or a clear failure. Because LLM backtests before the training cutoff are contaminated, that live record is the only clean evidence Oracle will ever have on its central design bet. It also means a model upgrade should be treated as a new, unvalidated analyst, which restarts the count.

The signal evidence points the same way. Edges that survive publication, costs and the loss of the short leg are slow, unglamorous and built from accounting data that Oracle can parse more carefully than most free-data users. Patience is a structural advantage over pods that are forced to de-gross together. So Oracle should not imitate Citadel's speed. Within its drawdown ladder and volatility target, it should hold slow positions through crowded unwinds and treat them as buying opportunities when its own slow signals agree. None of this is financial advice, and every expected premium here should be assumed to be smaller live than on paper.
